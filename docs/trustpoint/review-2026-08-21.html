A 19-minute health-disclosure call was scored 92.68 against a transcript containing no adviser

Diarisation returns a speaker count, and on 76 of 297 transcribed calls — 26% it comes back as one. Every one of those carries the whole call under a single label. Most are short (median 39 seconds: voicemails, no-answers) and harmless. Four are the wrap-up call of a scored sale:

Andrew Kerr is the worst sale in the tenant. Both voices are plainly present and readable in the text — and fused into a single Customer: turn with no Agent turn at all:

Customer: Hey, mister [NAME] Hi there, mister [NAME] It’s just [NAME] calling from Trust Point with regards to the life insurance. ← adviser Life insurance. Yeah. Well, I actually, I was up in neurology yesterday, which is good. And, basically, I’ve been given pretty much a clean bill of health. ← customer … So there’s nothing no sort of issues come up on all the tests? ← adviser Nothing come up at all. ← customer

Every “did the adviser say this” checkpoint on that sale was judged against a transcript in which the adviser does not exist as a speaker, on a call carrying a neurology referral, a viral infection and chronic fatigue. It scored 92.68.

Why it scored at all. A single-cluster call correctly bottoms out at 0.30 — but resolveSpeakerConfidence lifts a 'swapped' cleanup verdict to 0.75 unconditionally. That lift is justified on the reasoning that a model actively correcting labels is stating evidence, which assumes two labels to correct between. Here the “swap” flipped the one label the entire call sits under. 0.75 cleared the consent-gate floor of 0.5, so the consent gates auto-scored too.

Fixed — 21 Aug resolveSpeakerConfidence now takes the speaker count and refuses any lift below two clusters, with tests. A first attempt guarded the wrong function — identifyAdviserCluster abstains under two clusters, so that branch was already unreachable and the guard was dead code; it was reverted. Still open: the four sales keep their scores until quarantining lands, and reconstructing the turns is not the answer — see the note below.
Why the turns cannot be rebuilt Deepgram segmented Andrew Kerr’s call into 221 utterances and 79 paragraphs, so it knows where the turns change and only the who is missing — which looks like an opening. It is not. Role markers appear on 1.2% of utterances (3 of 253 across the affected calls; 3 of Andrew Kerr’s 221), so a deterministic assignment would be guessing at 98.8% of the call. A model pass could read it — but assessSpeakerIntegrity needs 6 markers to judge anything and these calls have 3, so the deterministic verifier returns “insufficient evidence” on exactly the transcripts needing verification. That would put unverifiable model-assigned speaker labels into a compliance record, which is the specific failure the integrity module exists to prevent.

D-08MediumRe-assessed 22 Aug — premise was wrong

Four mangled questions on the live UnderwriteMe profile set a trap for whoever confirms it

This finding originally said the four corrupt questions were inherited by every UnderwriteMe sale and could not match anything. That was wrong. Three facts it missed:

  • Items take their question text from the fresh parse, not from the stored profile — the profile is used for format matching and per-question rulings only.
  • Fresh parses are clean. The correct wordings resolve well across the tenant — Have you ever had any of these? 42 matches, Have you ever: 37. The corrupt strings produce zero items. The parser was fixed after 9 August; the profile holds the stale capture.
  • questions_vary = true on this profile, so drift detection is skipped — which is why 29 runs completed without complaint despite the snapshot no longer matching.

So there are no wrong outcomes today. What there is instead is a trap, and it is laid precisely where D-09 sends someone next.

reconcile.ts
const ruling = profileRulings.get(normaliseKey(pair.question));
const ruled = ruling?.checkMode ?? defaultCheckMode(pair.question);

A missed key falls back silently. And profileRulings is populated only when confirmed_by is set.

This profile went live by auto-corroboration, so nobody has confirmed it and the map is empty. The moment someone does confirm it, the rulings for those four become unreachable — normaliseKey("Have you : ever") will never match the "Have you ever:" a fresh parse produces. An operator sets check modes on the suicide, self-harm and mental-health questions, sees them saved, and the pipeline quietly uses heuristics instead.

The choices stored alongside each question say what they are, and all four sit in the mental-health block:

Fixed — 22 Aug The shape tests moved out of the diagnostic script into services/question-quality.ts and onto the profile-proposal path, which is the only moment they can prevent anything; a parse that mostly failed is now refused rather than offered to a human who can only reject it. A missed ruling key and two questions sharing one key both log loudly now instead of falling back in silence. The four questions are repaired by repair-profile-questions.ts, which derives each correction by matching word multisets against the wording the profile's own runs have stored — the corruption is span re-ordering, so the mangled and intact text carry the same words — and refuses rather than guesses when more than one candidate matches. Run it BEFORE confirming any profile under D-09.

Re-assessed 22 Aug. “Yield” was the wrong metric, and the fix this finding recommended would have been actively harmful. It measured value as how often the reviewer records a fail, treating a confirmed pass as wasted work. The queue's real value here is the opposite — it is catching the model's false fails:

Sub-floor items (confidence < 0.80)ItemsReviewer agreedOverturned
AI said PASS2612610 — 0.0%

Dropping the floor to 0.70 would have auto-scored 390 items and turned 153 of them into recorded failures a human had already overturned. The flip rate rises as confidence falls — 31% at 0.75, 50% at 0.70, 77% at 0.60 — so the queue is not wasteful, it is the mitigation for D-03's over-failing. Lowering it would have converted D-03 into 153 new breaches against advisers.

The confidence floor is nonetheless the right thing to look at: of the three mechanisms that route to review, it accounts for 489 of 787 on its own, and it is the weakest performer — 3.7% of its routings end in a recorded failure, against 13.6% for the speaker floor and 16.1% for a disputed checkpoint.

Fixed — 22 Aug The uncertainty is real but almost entirely one-sided, so the gate is now asymmetric below the floor. A low-confidence fail always goes to a person: it is wrong 94% of the time and it is an allegation against a named adviser. A low-confidence pass on an ordinary checkpoint auto-scores — it has not been overturned once in 261 reviews. Applied on both the journey and per-call paths so they cannot drift, and a caller supplying no verdict keeps the old symmetric behaviour.
Consent gates deliberately exempt They keep the symmetric floor whatever the sample says. 0 of 81 is a measurement of reviewer behaviour, not a proof, and a false pass on a consent is the worst output this product can produce. Net effect: 180 of 787 review items removed with zero measured risk, every catch kept — fewer than the 390 the dial change would have cleared, without manufacturing 153 false breaches to get there.
The caveat 0 of 180 measures reviewer behaviour, and D-03 found one instance of a reviewer passing a mandatory disclosure that is not in the transcript. So the true error rate on the pass side is not provably zero. 94% against 0% is too large a gap to be explained by that, but it is the assumption to revisit if reviewer quality is ever sampled properly.

Health questions are producing the accusation the schema was written to prevent

Migration 081’s premise is explicit: redaction strips health terms from transcripts, so their absence is not evidence a question was skipped, and reporting it as such “would put a false allegation against an adviser on the record, on the most serious questions in the application”. Two items break that rule on sale b4692141:

“What was the cause of your symptoms?” · application: “Chest infection” · asked_no_answer

Both are model-extracted items with call_answer_redacted = false, so they bypassed the redaction guard — the guard depends on the flag being set, and on a model-extracted run nothing sets it.

There may well be something real here: the reasoning notes the customer said “I’m healthy” and denied conditions while the application records a chest condition. But if that is the finding it is a mismatch, not “the adviser never asked”. The outcome asserts something about how the call was conducted, on the one class of question where the transcript cannot support it.

What to do Bar asked_no_answer and not_asked outright on health-category questions, regardless of extraction method — downgrade to mismatch where both sides have values, undetermined otherwise. Worth noting the deterministic path has never once emitted not_asked in 2,103 items; this leak is specific to model extraction.
