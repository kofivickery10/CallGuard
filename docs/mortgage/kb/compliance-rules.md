# Mortgage advice — compliance rules (MCOB + Consumer Duty)

Knowledge Base context for scoring **UK regulated mortgage advice** calls. This
is the sector baseline, derived from the FCA Handbook. It is deliberately
firm-agnostic: anything specific to a firm (fee amounts, lender panel, scripted
wording) belongs in `company-overview.md`, and the firm's own scripts belong in
the **Sales scripts** section.

Rule references are given so a breach can be explained to a compliance officer
in their own language.

---

## A. What must be disclosed, and when

The customer must know what kind of service they are getting **before** advice is
given — not once they are committed.

| Disclosure | When | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Adviser name, firm name, FCA authorisation | Opening | MCOB 4.4A |
| The range of mortgages covered: whole of market, a limited panel, or one lender | Before advice | MCOB 4.4A.1R |
| That the service is **advised** (a recommendation is being made) | Before advice | MCOB 4.4A |
| Any fee the customer pays: **amount, when it becomes payable, whether refundable** | Before advice | MCOB 4.4A.4R, MCOB 12 |
| That the firm may receive a commission / procuration fee from the lender | Before advice | MCOB 4.4A |
| Call recording and how the customer's data will be used | Opening | UK GDPR |
| Information will be shared with lenders and third parties for the application | Before it happens | UK GDPR |

**Fee disclosure is the item to be strictest about.** A fee mentioned only at
application stage, or described vaguely ("there may be a fee later"), is a
failure even where the amount is ultimately correct. The customer must be able to
decide whether to continue knowing what it costs.

## B. Know your customer, before recommending

A recommendation must rest on facts gathered on the call, not on assumptions
carried over from the enquiry (MCOB 4.7A.2R). Expect to hear:

- **Purpose and priorities** — purchase or remortgage, property, timescales, what
  matters most to this customer (lowest payment, certainty, flexibility, speed).
- **Income and employment** — including the basis: employed, self-employed,
  contract, bonus or commission-dependent.
- **Expenditure and existing credit commitments** — loans, cards, car finance.
- **Deposit or available equity**, and where the deposit is coming from.
- **Credit history** — missed payments, defaults, CCJs — before a lender is named.
- **Foreseeable changes** over the term: retirement, a planned income change,
  starting a family, reducing hours.

## C. Suitability — the reasoning must be spoken, not just documented

MCOB 4.7A requires the recommendation to be suitable and the customer to
understand *why*. A suitability letter written afterwards does not repair a call
where no reasoning was given.

**Repayment method** (MCOB 11.6.41). Capital repayment versus interest-only must
be a stated choice with a reason. Where interest-only is recommended there must
be a **credible repayment strategy**, stated on the call — not "we'll sort that
out later".

**Term.** Why this term, and the trade-off: a longer term lowers the monthly
payment and increases total interest paid.

**Term into retirement** (MCOB 11.6.14). Where the term runs to or beyond the
customer's expected retirement age, how the mortgage stays affordable in
retirement must be addressed.

**Product type.** Why fixed, tracker, discounted or variable suits *this*
customer's circumstances and tolerance for payment change.

**End of the initial rate period.** The customer must understand that the rate
reverts, that the payment will change, and that they will need to review. A
customer who does not know their payment will jump is the single most common
source of later complaints.

**Alternatives.** What else was considered and why it was not recommended.

**Product transfer versus remortgage.** On a rate review, staying with the
existing lender must be weighed against moving, including cost and effort of
each.

## D. Costs — the whole cost, not the monthly payment

Consumer Duty's *fair value* and *consumer understanding* outcomes both apply.

- **Total cost of borrowing** over the term or the deal period, not only the
  monthly figure. A low monthly payment concealing a high total cost is a fair
  value failure.
- **Early repayment charges** — that one exists, roughly how much, and for how
  long it applies.
- **Product / arrangement fee, valuation, legal and conveyancing costs**, and
  whether any are added to the loan (and therefore attract interest).
- **Overpayment allowances and portability**, where material to this customer.

## E. Affordability and vulnerability

**Affordability must be affirmatively checked with the customer** — the lender's
calculator passing is not the same as the customer confirming the payment is
sustainable for them (MCOB 11.6).

**Rate rises must be discussed.** What happens to the payment if rates rise.
Presenting the current payment as if it were permanent is a consumer
understanding failure.

**Vulnerability** (FG21/1, Consumer Duty). Indicators include health conditions,
recent bereavement, financial stress, low confidence or understanding, and time
pressure. The test is not whether the adviser recited a vulnerability script: it
is whether a cue the customer actually gave was **acknowledged and acted on**. A
customer who mentions a serious illness or a job loss and is moved straight past
it is a breach even if a vulnerability statement was read earlier.

## F. Related needs

- **Protection.** What would happen to the mortgage on death, serious illness or
  loss of income should be raised. Credit is for identifying and exploring the
  need — not for making a sale. An adviser who explores the need and the customer
  declines has done this correctly.
- **Buildings insurance** will be required as a condition of the mortgage and
  should be mentioned.

## G. Closing the call

- **Understanding checked and questions invited.** A closed "does that all make
  sense?" followed immediately by moving on does not evidence understanding.
- **Next steps, timescales and documentation** — including that written
  illustration and suitability documentation will follow.
- **No pressure or false urgency.** Genuine, factual deadlines are acceptable (a
  rate withdrawal the lender has already announced, an agreed completion date).
  Invented scarcity is not.
- **Consent to proceed and to a credit search** must be explicit. Silence is not
  consent.

---

## What is NOT a call check

These are real obligations, but they cannot be judged from a call recording and
should not be scored as though they can. They belong in file review:

- Whether the ESIS / illustration was actually issued, and within what timescale.
- Whether the suitability letter accurately reflects the advice given.
- Whether the lender's affordability assessment was correctly completed.
- Document retention and the contents of the customer file.

## Out of scope for this scorecard

**Equity release, lifetime mortgages and later-life lending** carry additional
requirements (MCOB 8, mandatory advice, the effect of rolled-up interest on the
estate, means-tested benefits, family involvement). They need their own scorecard
with manual-review gates — do not score them against this one.

**Buy-to-let** is only partly regulated: consumer buy-to-let is regulated, most
business buy-to-let is not. Confirm which applies before using this scorecard on
BTL calls.
